\label{sec:discussion}

The results support one methodological conclusion for the tested benchmark
classes: local differentiation and sparse solver policy are coupled numerical
choices. Across the benchmark suite, derivative routes, globalization, ownership
layout, and preconditioner policy affect cost and robustness even when terminal
observables agree. The remaining conclusions specify where this claim is
supported by the present evidence.

\paragraph{Derivative routes interact with the sparse solver.}
\Cref{fig:plasticity3d-derivative-ablation,tab:plasticity3d-derivative-ablation}
show a consistent cost comparison on the fixed $P_4(L_1)$,
$\lambda_{\mathrm{sr}}=1.5$, 8-rank case. All three routes reach essentially
the same terminal state with the same nonlinear and Krylov counts, so the
comparison is about derivative cost rather than about different converged
solutions. In this fixed test, constitutive AD preserves the same scalar-energy
definition while reducing wall time substantially relative to full element AD
and colored SFD.

\paragraph{Plasticity3D validation is endpoint-scoped.}
The Plasticity3D endpoint-surrogate comparison in
\Cref{fig:plasticity3d-validation,tab:plasticity3d-validation} supports a
scoped mechanics interpretation. The tested endpoint surrogate agrees closely
with the reported reference observables, and the fixed-$\lambda_{\mathrm{sr}}$
reference-operator comparison satisfies the endpoint field-agreement thresholds
under the same boundary conditions and active free degrees of freedom. The
evidence supports final-load scalar and field observables for the tested
endpoint surrogate, but it does not validate incremental plastic-history paths
or establish a numeric reproduction of the cited incremental-history studies.

\paragraph{External comparisons are useful when definitions are matched.}
The hyperelastic JAX-FEM comparison in
\Cref{fig:hyperelasticity-jaxfem-baseline,tab:hyperelasticity-jaxfem-baseline}
is informative because the mesh, prescribed displacement schedule, and
terminal-state energy evaluation are explicitly matched. The terminal states
are post-compared with the energy in
\cref{eq:hyperelasticity-energy}; the final energy relative difference is
\num{8.244e-6}, and the displacement and centerline relative differences are
below the prescribed 5\% threshold. Its role is solver-consistency evidence for
this hyperelastic benchmark: under a matched mesh, displacement schedule, and
terminal-energy post-comparison, the \jaxpetsc{} and JAX-FEM terminal states
agree closely.

\paragraph{Scope of the evidence.}
The same evidence also defines the scope of the conclusions. The
$\lambda_{\mathrm{sr}}=1.0$ Plasticity3D strong-scaling study is timing-only
evidence, while the bottom-clamped $\lambda_{\mathrm{sr}}=1.55$ scaling sweep is
the converged comparison tied to the benchmark. The topology study is reported
as end-to-end timing under adaptive stopping, with a separate controlled
rank-consistency check. The reference-operator PMG studies are
solver-policy diagnostics rather than symmetric external comparisons. These
distinctions define the contribution: a nonlinear FEM toolset with explicit
derivative-route choices, validation surfaces, and agreement limits. Automatic
differentiation alone does not determine the behavior of large nonlinear FEM;
globalization, sparse ownership, overlap replication, coarse-level policy,
preconditioning, and surrogate-model interpretation are part of the numerical
method.
