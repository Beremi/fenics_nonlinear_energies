\label{sec:discussion}

The analysis distinguishes a mathematical identity from the conditions needed
to realize it in distributed software. On a fixed smooth branch, element and
constitutive differentiation are two applications of the chain rule to the
same finite-dimensional potential. Colored recovery adds genuinely algebraic
requirements: a complete pattern, noninterfering same-color columns, consistent
overlap values, and unique ownership of every recovered row. Failure of any one
condition can preserve the scalar potential while corrupting the sparse
operator.

The finite-element experiments support both conditional statements on their
declared scopes. Element and constitutive routes agree across 15 prescribed
$P_1$, $P_2$, and $P_4$ states, with maximum residual and Hessian relative
discrepancies of $2.35\times10^{-16}$ and $2.34\times10^{-16}$. The separate
colored-recovery study covers $P_1(L_1)$ and $P_2(L_1)$, elastic and mixed
states, and one, two, and four ranks. Direct CSR comparisons pass in the four
one-rank blocks, and the maximum cross-rank action discrepancy is
$2.64\times10^{-16}$. These are strong regression tests for the stated discrete
objects, but they are not proofs for arbitrary meshes, constraint maps,
partitions, or future implementation changes.

The quadrature study shows why the discrete functional must be part of every
derivative claim. Relative to the 125-point reference, the degree-associated
$P_2(L_1)$ rule changes the potential scale by only
$7.11\times10^{-5}$, while the free residual and Hessian action change by
$3.43\times10^{-3}$ and $3.24\times10^{-2}$. For $P_4(L_1)$ the corresponding
values are $6.28\times10^{-4}$, $2.68\times10^{-2}$, and
$6.91\times10^{-2}$. No sampled $P_2$ point lies inside the declared
$10^{-8}$ switch band. The discrepancies therefore quantify fixed-state
quadrature sensitivity on the selected branches; they neither establish a
degree trend nor replace separately converged nonlinear endpoints.

The Mohr--Coulomb endpoint tests have an equally specific boundary. All five
selected labels are exercised at strict interior points, and the two-sided
interface, rotation, and repeated-spectrum probes remain finite under the
declared tie convention. Ordinary gradients and Hessians are used only within
branch interiors. No generalized derivative, semismooth convergence result,
incremental history validation, or physical slope-stability validation follows
from this surrogate.

The nonlinear studies illustrate why solver outcomes and method comparisons
must be reported separately. All 45 local stopping cases completed; 43 records
and 28 same-discretization comparisons pass their explicitly stated gates. The
seven cluster-deferred rows prevent a complete 52-row stopping-protocol pass.
These deterministic calibrations do not establish mesh convergence, a universal
stopping tolerance, or rank independence. In the 60-execution
globalization study, Newton--Armijo fails on all 15 Ginzburg--Landau
executions, whereas the reduced-trust method completes all 15; both methods
complete all 15 first-load hyperelastic executions. The Ginzburg--Landau pairs
never become eligible for endpoint comparison, and none of the 15
hyperelastic pairs passes the exact endpoint-identity condition. These counts
therefore support neither a method ordering nor a robustness probability.
Failure of the zero-tolerance hash gate also does not establish distinct
physical endpoint classes.

The checksum-verified workstation study verifies four-probe tangent-action
agreement rather than performance. It comprises 12 blocks and 36 sequential
route executions and limits the maximum action discrepancy to
$2.34\times10^{-16}$. No route timing enters the analysis, so the data support
no speedup, crossover, or route-ordering statement.

The independent manufactured problems verify consistency rather than physical
validity of the production benchmarks. The scalar solutions are smooth, the
Ginzburg--Landau verifier stays on a positive-curvature branch, and the
hyperelastic tests retain positive determinant. Except for the strictly convex
$p$-Laplace problem, small first-order residuals do not certify a local or
global minimizer. The main conclusion is therefore deliberately limited:
derivative placement can be changed without changing the tested discrete
operator when the mathematical and distributed contracts are both satisfied.
