\label{sec:discussion}

The numerical evidence supports a single methodological lesson for the tested
benchmark classes: local differentiation and sparse solver policy are coupled
numerical choices. Across the benchmark suite, derivative routes, globalization,
ownership layout, and preconditioner policy affect cost and robustness even when
terminal observables agree. The following paragraphs delimit that lesson by
derivative route, validation scope, and external comparison evidence.

\paragraph{Derivative routes interact with the sparse solver.}
\Cref{fig:plasticity3d-derivative-ablation,tab:plasticity3d-derivative-ablation}
show a consistent cost comparison on the fixed $P_4(L_1)$,
$\lambda_{\mathrm{sr}}=1.5$, 8-rank case. The three routes report terminal
observables that agree to the displayed precision, with 8 Newton iterations and 376 Krylov
iterations, so the comparison is about derivative cost rather than about
different converged solutions. In this fixed test, constitutive AD preserves the
same scalar-energy definition and has the lowest measured wall time:
\SI{222}{s}, compared with \SI{288}{s} for element AD and \SI{372}{s} for
colored SFD.

\paragraph{Three-dimensional plasticity validation is endpoint-scoped.}
The three-dimensional plasticity endpoint-surrogate comparison in
\Cref{fig:plasticity3d-validation,tab:plasticity3d-validation} supports a
scoped mechanics interpretation. The reported endpoint observables satisfy the
stated fixed-load tolerances under the matched boundary-condition setup. The
endpoint displacement is part of that fixed-load criterion, whereas the
deviatoric-strain and boundary-profile comparisons are diagnostic. The
evidence supports final-load scalar and field observables for the tested
endpoint surrogate, but it does not validate incremental plastic-history paths
or establish a numeric reproduction of the cited incremental-history studies.

\paragraph{External comparisons are useful when definitions are matched.}
The hyperelastic JAX-FEM comparison in
\Cref{fig:hyperelasticity-jaxfem-baseline,tab:hyperelasticity-jaxfem-baseline}
is informative because the mesh, prescribed displacement schedule, and
terminal-state energy evaluation are explicitly matched. The terminal states
are post-compared with the energy in
\cref{eq:hyperelasticity-energy}; the final energy relative difference is
\num{8.244e-6}, and the displacement and centerline relative differences are
below the prescribed 5\% threshold. Its role is solver-consistency evidence for
this hyperelastic benchmark: under a matched mesh, displacement schedule, and
terminal-energy post-comparison, the \jaxpetsc{} and JAX-FEM terminal states
agree closely.

\paragraph{Scope of the evidence.}
The same evidence also defines the scope of the conclusions. The
$\lambda_{\mathrm{sr}}=1.0$ three-dimensional plasticity strong-scaling study is
timing-only evidence, while the bottom-clamped $\lambda_{\mathrm{sr}}=1.55$
scaling sweep is the converged comparison tied to the benchmark. The topology
study is reported as end-to-end timing under adaptive stopping, with a separate
controlled rank-consistency check. The fixed-reference PMG studies are solver-policy
diagnostics rather than symmetric external comparisons. These
distinctions define the contribution: a nonlinear FEM toolset with explicit
derivative-route choices, matched validation comparisons, and stated limits of
agreement. Automatic
differentiation alone does not determine the behavior of large nonlinear FEM;
globalization, sparse ownership, overlap replication, coarse-level policy,
preconditioning, and surrogate-model interpretation are part of the numerical
method.
