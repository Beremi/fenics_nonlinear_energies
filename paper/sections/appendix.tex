\label{sec:solver-evidence}

\subsection{Complete endpoint-branch definition}
\label{subsec:mc-branch-definition}

This subsection records the exact scalar branch program used in
Section~\ref{sec:bench-plasticity3d}. Let $c_0$, $\phi$, and $\psi$ denote the
input cohesion, friction angle, and dilation angle. For strength-reduction
factor $\lambda_{\mathrm{sr}}>0$, the Davis--B map is
defined as in the stated slope-stability reduction context
\citep{davis1968plasticity,tschuchnigg2015nonassociated,sysala2021optimization}:
\begin{align}
  c_1&:=\frac{c_0}{\lambda_{\mathrm{sr}}},
  &
  \phi_1&:=\arctan\!\left(
      \frac{\tan\phi}{\lambda_{\mathrm{sr}}}\right),
  &
  \psi_1&:=\arctan\!\left(
      \frac{\tan\psi}{\lambda_{\mathrm{sr}}}\right),
  \nonumber\\
  \beta&:=\frac{\cos\phi_1\cos\psi_1}
                 {1-\sin\phi_1\sin\psi_1},
  &
  c_\lambda&:=\beta c_1,
  &
  \phi_\lambda&:=\arctan(\beta\tan\phi_1),
  \label{eq:appendix-davis-b}
\end{align}
with
\begin{equation}
  \bar c_\lambda:=2c_\lambda\cos\phi_\lambda,
  \qquad s_\lambda:=\sin\phi_\lambda.
  \label{eq:appendix-reduced-parameters}
\end{equation}
The tested material table has $\psi=0$, but the implementation retains the
complete map \eqref{eq:appendix-davis-b}.
The mathematical domain used below has finite ordered principal strains,
$K>0$, $\mu>0$, $-1<s_\lambda<1$, and $s_\lambda\ne0$ whenever the apex row is
reachable. On this domain the displayed denominators are nonzero. Defensive
tiny-denominator substitutions in the program are failure containment outside
this model; an admitted row must instead pass the stored denominator-margin
gate.

Let $I_1:=\operatorname{tr}\varepsilon$ and
\begin{equation}
  Q(\varepsilon)
  :=\varepsilon_{xx}^2+\varepsilon_{yy}^2+\varepsilon_{zz}^2
  +\frac12\left(\gamma_{xy}^2+\gamma_{yz}^2+\gamma_{xz}^2\right).
  \label{eq:appendix-engineering-quadratic}
\end{equation}
The elastic energy is
\begin{equation}
  \Phi_{\mathrm{el}}
  :=\frac12\lambda_{\mathrm L}I_1^2+\mu Q(\varepsilon).
  \label{eq:appendix-elastic-branch}
\end{equation}
In addition to $f_{\mathrm{tr}}$ from
\eqref{eq:plasticity3d-trial-function}, the return tests are
\begin{align}
  r_{\mathrm{sl}}&:=\frac{\varepsilon_1-\varepsilon_2}{1+s_\lambda},
  &
  r_{\mathrm{sr}}&:=\frac{\varepsilon_2-\varepsilon_3}{1-s_\lambda},
  \nonumber\\
  r_{\mathrm{la}}&:=
    \frac{\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2-2\varepsilon_3}{3-s_\lambda},
  &
  r_{\mathrm{ra}}&:=
    \frac{2\varepsilon_1-\varepsilon_2-\varepsilon_3}{3+s_\lambda}.
  \label{eq:appendix-return-tests}
\end{align}
Their strictly positive denominators are
\begin{align}
  D_{\mathrm s}
  &:=4\lambda_{\mathrm L}s_\lambda^2+4\mu(1+s_\lambda^2),
  \nonumber\\
  D_{\mathrm l}
  &:=4\lambda_{\mathrm L}s_\lambda^2
    +\mu(1+s_\lambda)^2+2\mu(1-s_\lambda)^2,
  \nonumber\\
  D_{\mathrm r}
  &:=4\lambda_{\mathrm L}s_\lambda^2
    +2\mu(1+s_\lambda)^2+\mu(1-s_\lambda)^2,
  \nonumber\\
  D_{\mathrm a}
  &:=4Ks_\lambda^2.
  \label{eq:appendix-branch-denominators}
\end{align}
For the tested parameters $s_\lambda>0$; the verifier records normalized
denominator margins.

The four plastic multipliers are
\begin{align}
  \Lambda_{\mathrm s}
  &:=\frac{f_{\mathrm{tr}}}{D_{\mathrm s}},
  \nonumber\\
  \Lambda_{\mathrm l}
  &:=\frac{
    \mu\bigl((1+s_\lambda)(\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2)
    -2(1-s_\lambda)\varepsilon_3\bigr)
    +2\lambda_{\mathrm L}s_\lambda I_1-\bar c_\lambda}
    {D_{\mathrm l}},
  \nonumber\\
  \Lambda_{\mathrm r}
  &:=\frac{
    \mu\bigl(2(1+s_\lambda)\varepsilon_1
    -(1-s_\lambda)(\varepsilon_2+\varepsilon_3)\bigr)
    +2\lambda_{\mathrm L}s_\lambda I_1-\bar c_\lambda}
    {D_{\mathrm r}},
  \nonumber\\
  \Lambda_{\mathrm a}
  &:=\frac{2Ks_\lambda I_1-\bar c_\lambda}{D_{\mathrm a}}.
  \label{eq:appendix-branch-multipliers}
\end{align}
The associated energies are
\begin{align}
  \Phi_{\mathrm s}
  &:=\Phi_{\mathrm{el}}
    -\frac12D_{\mathrm s}\Lambda_{\mathrm s}^2,
  \nonumber\\
  \Phi_{\mathrm l}
  &:=\frac12\lambda_{\mathrm L}I_1^2
    +\mu\left(\varepsilon_3^2
      +\frac12(\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2)^2\right)
    -\frac12D_{\mathrm l}\Lambda_{\mathrm l}^2,
  \nonumber\\
  \Phi_{\mathrm r}
  &:=\frac12\lambda_{\mathrm L}I_1^2
    +\mu\left(\varepsilon_1^2
      +\frac12(\varepsilon_2+\varepsilon_3)^2\right)
    -\frac12D_{\mathrm r}\Lambda_{\mathrm r}^2,
  \nonumber\\
  \Phi_{\mathrm a}
  &:=\frac12KI_1^2
    -\frac12D_{\mathrm a}\Lambda_{\mathrm a}^2.
  \label{eq:appendix-branch-energies}
\end{align}
The elastic-first selection order is
\begin{equation}
  \Phi_{\mathrm{MC}}(\varepsilon)
  :=
  \begin{cases}
    \Phi_{\mathrm{el}},
      & f_{\mathrm{tr}}\le0,\\
    \Phi_{\mathrm s},
      & f_{\mathrm{tr}}>0\ \text{and}\ \Lambda_{\mathrm s}\le
        \min(r_{\mathrm{sl}},r_{\mathrm{sr}}),\\
    \Phi_{\mathrm l},
      & f_{\mathrm{tr}}>0\ \text{and}\ r_{\mathrm{sl}}<r_{\mathrm{sr}}
        \quad\text{and}\quad
        r_{\mathrm{sl}}\le\Lambda_{\mathrm l}\le r_{\mathrm{la}},\\
    \Phi_{\mathrm r},
      & f_{\mathrm{tr}}>0\ \text{and}\ r_{\mathrm{sr}}<r_{\mathrm{sl}}
        \quad\text{and}\quad
        r_{\mathrm{sr}}\le\Lambda_{\mathrm r}\le r_{\mathrm{ra}},\\
    \Phi_{\mathrm a},
      & f_{\mathrm{tr}}>0\ \text{and none of the three tests above holds},
  \end{cases}
  .
  \label{eq:appendix-branch-selection}
\end{equation}
The cases are evaluated from top to bottom and the first true row is returned;
mutual exclusivity of the un-ordered plastic predicates is therefore not
assumed. The predicates are part of the discrete surrogate. Differentiation
treats the selected row of \eqref{eq:appendix-branch-selection} as fixed.

\subsection{Interface and repeated-spectrum probes}
\label{subsec:interface-probes}

For every adjacent branch pair, the material-point verifier constructs states
on both sides of the switching predicate. Each state records the normalized
active-branch margin, constitutive denominator, principal-value gap, finite
stress and tangent, tangent symmetry, and centered finite-difference action.
The two one-sided selected Hessians are compared descriptively. Their difference
does not define a generalized derivative.

Repeated principal values are evaluated under the deterministic diagonal
perturbation stated in Section~\ref{sec:bench-plasticity3d}. The checks require
finite energy, stress, and tangent, but they do not claim rotational invariance
of the arbitrary tie-selected tangent at exact degeneracy. Separate rotations
of strict interior states test energy invariance and stress/tangent covariance.

\subsection{Stopping-study scope}
\label{subsec:stopping-study-scope}

The local stopping matrix contains 45 checksum-identified cases. Its parameter
grid is as follows: Ginzburg--Landau relative residual targets
$10^{-2},10^{-4},10^{-6},10^{-8}$ on $L_5$ and $L_6$; hyperelastic Riesz-solve
relative tolerances $10^{-8},10^{-10},10^{-12}$ on $L_1$ and $L_2$;
hyperelastic nonlinear relative residual targets
$10^{-2},10^{-4},10^{-6},10^{-8}$ on the same two levels; fixed-state
endpoint-surrogate Krylov tolerances $10^{-2},10^{-4},10^{-6},10^{-8},10^{-10}$
for degrees one, two, and four; and endpoint-surrogate nonlinear relative
residual targets $10^{-2},10^{-4},10^{-6},10^{-7}$ for degrees one and two.
The reported 45-row local subset contains no degree-four nonlinear endpoint
and supports no inference about rank-count consistency. The four
degree-four nonlinear rows and three MPI-consistency rows in the 52-row frozen
plan remain cluster-deferred.

For every mesh/discretization group, let $x_\star$ denote the result at the
tightest computed tolerance and $x_\tau$ a candidate. When a scalar relative
difference is required, we use
\begin{equation}
  d_{\mathrm{rel}}(a,b)
  :=\frac{|a-b|}{\max(|a|,|b|,\operatorname{tiny}_{64})}.
  \label{eq:stopping-scalar-relative-difference}
\end{equation}
For Ginzburg--Landau, the same-mesh state discrepancy is
\begin{equation}
  \eta_{L_h^2}
  :=\frac{\bigl[\sum_i m_i(u_{\tau,i}-u_{\star,i})^2\bigr]^{1/2}}
          {\max\!\left(
           [\sum_i m_i u_{\star,i}^2]^{1/2},
           \operatorname{tiny}_{64}\right)},
  \qquad
  m_i:=\sum_{T\ni i}\frac{|T|}{3}.
  \label{eq:stopping-lumped-state-difference}
\end{equation}
For mechanics, with the frozen reference-elastic Riesz operator
$K_{\mathrm{ref}}$, the state discrepancy is
\begin{equation}
  \eta_K
  :=\frac{\bigl[(x_\tau-x_\star)^\top K_{\mathrm{ref}}
                    (x_\tau-x_\star)\bigr]^{1/2}}
          {\max\!\left(
           [x_\star^\top K_{\mathrm{ref}}x_\star]^{1/2},
           \operatorname{tiny}_{64}\right)}.
  \label{eq:stopping-riesz-state-difference}
\end{equation}
The fixed-state linear comparison applies the same formula to the correction
vectors. The coefficient-space discrepancy is
\begin{equation}
  \eta_2(v_\tau,v_\star)
  :=\frac{\lVert v_\tau-v_\star\rVert_2}
          {\max(\lVert v_\star\rVert_2,\operatorname{tiny}_{64})},
  \label{eq:stopping-coefficient-state-difference}
\end{equation}
where $v$ is the displacement array for a hyperelastic endpoint and the
correction vector for a fixed-state endpoint-surrogate solve. We write
$\Pi_\tau:=\Pi_h(x_\tau)$, $\Pi_\star:=\Pi_h(x_\star)$,
$\omega_\tau:=f_h^\top x_\tau$, and $\omega_\star:=f_h^\top x_\star$. The
maximum nodal displacements are
$u_{\max,\tau}:=\max_i\lVert\boldsymbol{u}_{\tau,i}\rVert_2$ and
$u_{\max,\star}:=\max_i\lVert\boldsymbol{u}_{\star,i}\rVert_2$.

Record admission is distinct from comparison admission. Ginzburg--Landau
requires one successful solve/load record, the lumped-$L^2$ residual metric,
and a stored relative residual independently checked against its row target.
Every
reference-elastic norm solve uses conjugate gradients with Jacobi
preconditioning, the unpreconditioned residual norm, zero absolute tolerance,
and a cap of 5,000 iterations; the requested and effective settings must agree,
and runtime options may not alter them. Hyperelastic Riesz-setup rows require a
positive numerical-inertia certificate for $K_{\mathrm{ref}}$, a positive
Krylov convergence reason, and a relative true residual no larger than
$10^{-6}$. Hyperelastic nonlinear and nonlinear endpoint-surrogate rows require
the same certificate and convergence reason with a true-residual gate of
$10^{-8}$. A nonlinear endpoint must also satisfy
$R_k/R_0\leq\tau_R^{\mathrm{rel}}$. A fixed-state endpoint-surrogate row requires a positive
Krylov reason, branch margin at least $10^{-8}$, zero quadrature fraction in the
switch band, and relative true residual no larger than
$\max(10^{-12},20\tau_{\mathrm{Krylov}})$.

Subject to endpoint admission, every condition in the applicable comparison
row below is conjunctive:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Ginzburg--Landau requires $\eta_{L_h^2}\leq10^{-5}$ and
        $|\Pi_\tau-\Pi_\star|\leq10^{-10}$.
  \item A hyperelastic Riesz setup check requires relative dual-norm and
        state-scale differences no larger than $10^{-6}$ and $10^{-12}$,
        respectively, and a relative true residual no larger than
        $\max(10^{-8},20\tau_{\mathrm{Riesz}})$.
  \item A hyperelastic nonlinear endpoint requires $\eta_2$ and $\eta_K$ to be
        no larger than $10^{-5}$,
        and $|\Pi_\tau-\Pi_\star|\leq10^{-8}$.
  \item A fixed-state endpoint-surrogate solve requires $\eta_2$ and the
        reference-elastic relative correction discrepancy to be no larger than
        $10^{-4}$, and a relative true residual no larger than
        $\max(10^{-10},20\tau_{\mathrm{Krylov}})$.
  \item A nonlinear endpoint-surrogate solve requires
        $\eta_K\leq10^{-5}$,
        $|\Pi_\tau-\Pi_\star|\leq10^{-6}$,
        $|\omega_\tau-\omega_\star|\leq10^{-6}$,
        $|u_{\max,\tau}-u_{\max,\star}|\leq10^{-8}$, and exact equality of
        the complete branch-count dictionaries.
\end{enumerate}

The two inadmissible records are degree-four fixed-state solves. Their
independent relative true residuals are $0.305>0.2$ at
$\tau_{\mathrm{Krylov}}=10^{-2}$ and
$2.20\times10^{-5}>2.00\times10^{-5}$ at
$\tau_{\mathrm{Krylov}}=10^{-6}$. Among the 15 rejected but comparable rows,
the failed-predicate incidences are: Ginzburg--Landau state 2 and energy 3;
hyperelastic nonlinear coefficient, Riesz-state, and energy 2 each;
fixed-state surrogate coefficient and Riesz-correction 5 each; and nonlinear
surrogate Riesz-state 2, energy 4, external work 5, maximum displacement 4,
and branch counts 3. These incidences are nonexclusive because one row may
fail several predicates. Thus the local study calibrates deterministic
same-discretization accuracy only; it is neither a mesh-convergence study nor a
timing, scaling, or population-robustness experiment.

\subsection{Numerical configurations}
\label{subsec:numerical-configurations}

The scalar manufactured meshes have $8$, $16$, $32$, and $64$ uniform
subdivisions per coordinate direction. The $p$-Laplace solve starts from the
affine extension $x+y$ and the Ginzburg--Landau solve starts from its exact
Dirichlet extension; both use damped Newton iteration and the relative residual
defined in Section~\ref{subsec:verification-normalization}. The nonaffine
hyperelastic meshes and material constants are stated in
Section~\ref{sec:bench-he}. The affine patch uses one unit cube divided into six
tetrahedra.

The controlled zero-source Ginzburg--Landau study uses the level-5
$P_1$ mesh of $[-1,1]^2$, with 4,225 nodes, 8,192 triangles, and 3,969 free
coefficients. It sets $\epsilon=0.01$, $f=0$, and $u=0$ on the complete
boundary. The nominal free state is
\begin{equation}
  u_0(x,y):=\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi(x-1)}{2}\right)
             \sin\!\left(\frac{\pi(y-1)}{2}\right).
  \label{eq:gl-globalization-initial-state}
\end{equation}
The two additional starts are
$u_0\pm0.025\,m_{\mathrm{GL}}$, where
\begin{equation}
  m_{\mathrm{GL}}(x,y)
  :=\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi(x+1)}{2}\right)
    \sin\!\left(\frac{\pi(y+1)}{2}\right)
    \sin\!\bigl(\pi(x+1)\bigr)
    \sin\!\bigl(\pi(y+1)\bigr).
  \label{eq:gl-globalization-perturbation}
\end{equation}
The perturbation is applied only to free nodes and vanishes on the boundary.
Each state is stored once in a checksum-identified NumPy archive and is shared
by both methods and all five executions. The comparison uses two MPI ranks,
one thread per rank, element Hessians, GMRES with hypre preconditioning,
relative tolerance $10^{-3}$ and cap 200, and at most 100 nonlinear iterations.
The three starts are deterministic sensitivity cases rather than population
samples.

The distributed hyperelastic fixed-state test uses the brick
$[0,0.4]\times[-0.005,0.005]^2$, an $80\times2\times2$ cell grid split into
1,920 tetrahedra, 729 nodes, and 2,133 free vector DOFs after both end faces are
constrained. Its constants are
$C_1=3.8462\times10^7$ and $D_1=8.3333\times10^7$ in the nondimensional stress
scale of Section~\ref{sec:bench-he}; run records retain the full binary64
values. The stored fixed state twists the right face by $0.15$ radians and is
assembled independently on one, two, and four ranks; no nonlinear trajectory is
inferred from that state.

The branch-structured endpoint benchmark uses the $L_1$ macro mesh with 3,845
vertices and 18,419 tetrahedra. Its bounding box is
$[-175,30]\times[0,60]\times[0,86.6025403784]$ in the scaled coordinates of
Section~\ref{sec:bench-he}. Define
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \Gamma_b&:=\partial\Omega\cap\{y=0\},\\
    \Gamma_x^-&:=\partial\Omega\cap\{x=-175\},&
    \Gamma_x^+&:=\partial\Omega\cap\{x=30\},\\
    \Gamma_z^-&:=\partial\Omega\cap\{z=0\},&
    \Gamma_z^+&:=\partial\Omega\cap\{z=86.6025403784\}.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:endpoint-surrogate-boundaries}
\end{equation}
For $\boldsymbol{u}=(u_x,u_y,u_z)$, the homogeneous essential conditions are
\begin{equation}
  \boldsymbol{u}=\boldsymbol{0}\ \text{on }\Gamma_b,\qquad
  u_x=0\ \text{on }\Gamma_x^-\cup\Gamma_x^+,\qquad
  u_z=0\ \text{on }\Gamma_z^-\cup\Gamma_z^+.
  \label{eq:endpoint-surrogate-boundary-conditions}
\end{equation}
All remaining displacement components are free. No surface-load term is
applied; hence the fully free boundary has zero natural traction, and the
unconstrained components of the roller planes have zero natural tangential
traction. The body force is
$\boldsymbol{b}(X)=-\gamma(X)\boldsymbol{e}_y$, with $\gamma$ piecewise
constant by material region. All prescribed displacement values vanish, so
the affine lift is zero.

The $P_1$, $P_2$, and $P_4$ constrained spaces have 10,526, 79,024, and
610,964 free vector DOFs. The four material rows are given in
