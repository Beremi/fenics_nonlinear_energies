\label{sec:conclusion}

We established a conditional equivalence framework for three placements of
automatic differentiation in nonlinear finite elements. Element and
constitutive differentiation agree when they act on the same quadrature-defined
potential, affine constraint map, strain convention, and smooth active branch.
Colored sparse recovery produces the same operator only when the pattern,
interference coloring, overlap state, and global row ownership are also
consistent.

The checksum-verified numerical evidence supports these statements on the
tested problems.
Element and constitutive routes agree to binary64 precision across 15 prescribed
linear and high-order states. Distributed colored recovery passes the prescribed
tangent-action checks for $P_1(L_1)$ and $P_2(L_1)$ elastic and mixed states on
one, two, and four ranks; direct CSR comparisons pass at one rank.
Independent manufactured and analytic checks recover the expected scalar
finite-element behavior and verify smooth hyperelastic stresses and tangents.
The branch program passes its interior, interface, rotation, and repeated-
spectrum checks within the explicit exclusion of ordinary Hessians at switches.

The same evidence also identifies the limits of the conclusions. Fixed-state
quadrature changes residuals and Hessian actions much more than the potential
scale for $P_2$ and $P_4$. The completed 45-row local stopping matrix calibrates
only deterministic same-discretization accuracy; seven cluster-deferred rows
prevent a complete stopping-protocol pass. In the globalization study, the
Ginzburg--Landau pairs do not reach endpoint comparison and none of the
hyperelastic pairs passes exact endpoint identity. The workstation route study
passes its state, common-residual, four-probe action, and provenance checks, but
no route timing enters the analysis. Consequently, we claim no fastest
derivative route, crossover predictor, nonlinear robustness probability, or
parallel scaling law.
