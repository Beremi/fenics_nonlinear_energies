We developed a nonlinear finite-element energy-minimization toolset, realized
primarily through local JAX kernels and PETSc sparse distributed solvers, for
studying derivative construction and solver policy as coupled numerical choices.
Across the tested benchmark suite, the construction links
scalar energy definitions, derivative-route selection, sparse ownership, and
validation comparisons within one distributed solver setting. The tested
problems show that differentiable local modeling and distributed nonlinear
solvers interact at the level of numerical-method design.

The numerical results support this pattern on a deliberately heterogeneous
benchmark suite. Scalar problems check formulation and derivative agreement,
hyperelasticity provides a tightly matched external comparison, three-dimensional
plasticity tests nonsmooth constitutive structure and distributed sparse solves
under an endpoint-surrogate interpretation, and topology optimization exercises a
coupled design-and-mechanics problem. In the fixed high-order three-dimensional
plasticity derivative-route comparison, constitutive AD is the lowest measured
wall-time route among the tested alternatives. In the reported $P_4(L_2)$,
$\lambda_{\mathrm{sr}}=\num{1.55}$ three-dimensional plasticity scaling sweep,
all rank counts converge with the same nonlinear and Krylov work. The topology
runs provide distributed timing evidence for the coupled design-and-mechanics
loop, with a separate controlled rank-consistency check.

Natural extensions are path-dependent mechanics validation against
incremental-history references, broader agreement-focused external comparisons
under matched problem definitions, and reduced preconditioner-setup and
multigrid costs in the largest sparse mechanics runs in this study. Together,
these directions extend the validation discipline of the present study toward
broader nonlinear multiphysics and design problems.
