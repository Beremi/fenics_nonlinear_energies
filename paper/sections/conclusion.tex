This paper developed a \jaxpetsc{} toolset that connects local JAX
differentiation with PETSc sparse distributed assembly, Newton globalization,
Krylov solvers, and preconditioner policy for nonlinear finite-element energy
minimization. Across the tested benchmark suite, the construction links scalar
energy definitions, derivative-route selection, sparse ownership, and validation
comparisons within one distributed solver setting. The tested problems show
that differentiable local modeling and distributed nonlinear solvers interact at
the level of numerical-method design.

The numerical results support this pattern on a deliberately heterogeneous
benchmark suite. Scalar problems check formulation and derivative agreement,
hyperelasticity provides a tightly matched external comparison, Plasticity3D
tests nonsmooth constitutive structure and distributed sparse solves under an
endpoint-surrogate interpretation, and topology optimization exercises a
coupled design-and-mechanics problem. In the fixed high-order Plasticity3D
derivative-route comparison, constitutive AD is the lowest measured wall-time
route among the tested alternatives. In the reported $P_4(L_2)$,
$\lambda_{\mathrm{sr}}=\num{1.55}$ Plasticity3D scaling sweep, all rank counts
converge with the same nonlinear and Krylov work. The topology runs demonstrate
end-to-end distributed design-mechanics timing with a separate controlled
rank-consistency check.

These results point to three scientific extensions. The first is
path-dependent mechanics validation against an incremental-history reference.
The second is a wider set of agreement-focused external comparisons under
matched problem definitions. The third is continued reduction of setup and
multigrid costs in the largest sparse mechanics runs. Together, these
directions extend the validation discipline of the present study toward broader
nonlinear multiphysics and design problems.
