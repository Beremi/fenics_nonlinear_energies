\begin{abstract}
Large nonlinear finite-element energy problems require reliable local
derivatives, robust nonlinear globalization, and sparse distributed linear
algebra that is matched to the mechanics of the problem. We develop a
scientific toolset for studying these requirements as coupled numerical choices
in nonlinear finite-element energy minimization and energy-based topology
optimization. The primary \jaxpetsc{} realization uses JAX for local energy and
constitutive kernels and PETSc for sparse assembly, Newton globalization,
Krylov solvers, and preconditioners. The benchmark suite covers
$p$-Laplace, Ginzburg--Landau, finite-strain hyperelasticity, two- and
three-dimensional Mohr--Coulomb plasticity, and topology optimization.
Where available, \purejax{} and \fenics{} runs serve as internal reference
implementations; the JAX-FEM and reference-model comparisons are restricted to
matched benchmark definitions and observables.

Across these problems, we compare element automatic differentiation,
constitutive automatic differentiation, and colored sparse finite-difference
recovery as routes to second-order information. The validation checks report
matched hyperelastic energy and field agreement within the stated tolerances and
final-load scalar and field agreement for the three-dimensional plasticity
endpoint surrogate. The performance studies then quantify how derivative routes, sparse ownership,
nonlinear globalization, Krylov linear algebra, and preconditioner policy affect
cost and robustness under those definitions. On the tested Plasticity3D
derivative-route case, constitutive AD gives the lowest measured wall time while
preserving terminal observables, and the larger mechanics and topology runs
exercise the same sparse-solver design at distributed scale.
\end{abstract}
