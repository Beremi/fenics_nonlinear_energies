\begin{abstract}
Second derivatives of nonlinear finite-element energies may be constructed at
the element, constitutive, or assembled sparse-operator level. Element and
constitutive differentiation are mathematically equivalent when they
differentiate the same quadrature functional, affine constraint map, and smooth
constitutive branch. Colored recovery yields the same sparse operator under the
additional requirements of a complete structural pattern, a valid column-
interference coloring, consistent overlap values, and unique global row
ownership. We state these conditional results and implement the three routes
with JAX and PETSc.

The numerical study separates derivative correctness from nonlinear and
performance claims. Across 15 prescribed $P_1$, $P_2$, and $P_4$ states, the
largest element-versus-constitutive residual and Hessian discrepancies are
$2.35\times10^{-16}$ and $2.34\times10^{-16}$. For distributed colored
recovery, prescribed tangent actions agree on elastic and mixed states over
one, two, and four Message Passing Interface (MPI) ranks; the largest
cross-rank discrepancy is $2.64\times10^{-16}$, and direct compressed sparse
row (CSR) comparisons pass at one rank.
Material-point tests cover all five branches of a three-dimensional
Mohr--Coulomb endpoint surrogate while excluding ordinary Hessian claims at
switches. Independent scalar and hyperelastic manufactured problems recover
the expected linear-element spatial behavior.

Fixed-state quadrature comparisons show that small potential differences can
coexist with appreciable residual and Hessian-action differences; for
degree-four elements on the base mesh, the latter reach $2.68\%$ and
$6.91\%$, respectively. The results
therefore support the conditional derivative identities and distributed
construction on the tested states. We make no universal route-ranking,
crossover, timing, or scaling claim.
\end{abstract}
