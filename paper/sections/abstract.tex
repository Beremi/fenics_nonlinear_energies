\begin{abstract}
Large nonlinear finite-element energy problems require reliable local
derivatives, robust nonlinear globalization, and sparse distributed linear
algebra that is matched to the mechanics of the problem. We develop a
computational toolset for studying these requirements together in nonlinear
finite-element solves and energy-based topology optimization. The primary
realization couples JAX local energy and constitutive kernels with PETSc sparse
assembly, Newton globalization, Krylov solvers, and preconditioners. The
\purejax{} and \fenics{} formulations provide scoped reference implementations,
and external comparisons are restricted to matched observables. The benchmark suite
covers $p$-Laplace, Ginzburg--Landau, finite-strain hyperelasticity, two- and
three-dimensional Mohr--Coulomb plasticity, and topology optimization.

Across these problems, we compare element automatic differentiation,
constitutive automatic differentiation, and colored sparse finite-difference
recovery as routes to second-order information. The evidence separates
agreement checks from performance claims: the hyperelastic benchmark satisfies
matched energy and field criteria, the three-dimensional plasticity comparison
is restricted to final-load scalar and field observables for the endpoint
surrogate, and the timing studies characterize derivative-route and
solver-policy costs under those definitions. On the tested Plasticity3D
derivative-route case, constitutive AD gives the lowest measured wall time while
preserving terminal observables; the larger mechanics and topology runs exercise
the same sparse-solver design at distributed scale. For the tested benchmark
classes, the results show that derivative construction, sparse ownership,
nonlinear globalization, and preconditioner policy are coupled numerical choices
for distributed nonlinear FEM.
\end{abstract}
