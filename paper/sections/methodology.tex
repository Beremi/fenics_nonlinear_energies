\label{sec:methodology}

We first define the algebraic object differentiated by every route. Let
$V_{h,0}=\operatorname{span}\{\varphi_i\}_{i=1}^{n}$ be the finite-element
space satisfying homogeneous essential conditions, and let
$x\in\mathbb{R}^n$ contain its free scalar coefficients. Essential data enter
through an affine lift $\bar u_h(\theta)$, where $\theta$ collects fixed load
and material parameters. The corresponding state is
\begin{equation}
  u_h(x;\theta)
  =\bar u_h(\theta)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_i\varphi_i.
  \label{eq:constrained-state}
\end{equation}
For element $e$, let $R_e$ restrict the global free vector to the coefficients
whose basis functions intersect $e$, and let $L_e$ place these coefficients
in the chosen local component ordering. We define
\begin{equation}
  u_e(x;\theta)=L_e R_e x+b_e(\theta)=:A_e x+b_e(\theta),
  \qquad A_e:=L_eR_e,
  \label{eq:element-affine-map}
\end{equation}
where $b_e(\theta)$ contains the element coefficients of the affine lift.
Thus $A_e$ is fixed for a given constrained space and includes arbitrary
free-DOF permutations without assigning coefficients to constrained DOFs.

Let $B_{eq}$ be the fixed linear map from the local coefficients to the complete
quadrature-point argument required by the density. This argument may stack a
field value and its gradient, as for Ginzburg--Landau, or reduce to a strain or
deformation gradient in mechanics. We fix the quadrature rule, weights, local
material data $m_{eq}$, and a deterministic first-match selector
$\beta_{eq}(x;\theta)$ evaluated from this argument and the material data. We
define the local quadrature jet
\begin{equation}
  z_{eq}(x;\theta)
  :=B_{eq}\bigl(A_e x+b_e(\theta)\bigr).
  \label{eq:quadrature-jet}
\end{equation}
If $\mathcal{U}_{\alpha}(m)$ denotes the domain of constitutive branch
$\alpha$, we take the admissible free-space domain to be
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{D}_h(\theta)
  :=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^n\;\middle|\;
  \begin{aligned}
    &\beta_{eq}(x;\theta)\ \text{is defined},\quad
      z_{eq}(x;\theta)
      \in\mathcal{U}_{\beta_{eq}(x;\theta)}(m_{eq}),\\
    &\mathcal{W}_{\beta_{eq}(x;\theta)}
      \bigl(z_{eq}(x;\theta);m_{eq}\bigr)\in\mathbb{R}
      \quad\text{for every }(e,q)
  \end{aligned}\right\}.
  \label{eq:admissible-domain}
\end{equation}
This definition includes model-specific restrictions, such as positive
deformation Jacobians in hyperelasticity. The discrete element contribution is
\begin{equation}
  \Phi_e(x;\theta)
  :=\sum_{q=1}^{n_q}w_{eq}
  \mathcal{W}_{\beta_{eq}(x;\theta)}
  \bigl(z_{eq}(x;\theta);m_{eq}\bigr),
  \label{eq:element-quadrature-potential}
\end{equation}
and the global free-space potential is
\begin{equation}
  \Pi_h(x;\theta)
  :=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{T}_h}\Phi_e(x;\theta)-f_h(\theta)^\top x.
  \label{eq:generic-potential}
\end{equation}
Here the linear external functional $\ell_h(\,\cdot\,;\theta)$ is represented
on the free basis by
$(f_h(\theta))_i:=\ell_h(\varphi_i;\theta)$. Its value on the affine lift is
independent of $x$ and may therefore be omitted together with any other
additive term that depends only on prescribed data. The discrete optimization
problem is
\begin{equation}
  \min_{x\in\mathcal{D}_h(\theta)}\Pi_h(x;\theta).
  \label{eq:discrete-optimization-problem}
\end{equation}
On an open subset of $\mathcal{D}_h(\theta)$ where the selected branches are
twice continuously differentiable, we define
\begin{equation}
  g_h(x;\theta):=\nabla_x\Pi_h(x;\theta),
  \qquad H_h(x;\theta):=\nabla_x^2\Pi_h(x;\theta).
  \label{eq:global-gradient-hessian}
\end{equation}
The algorithms seek admissible stationary points, characterized by
$g_h(x;\theta)=0$ at branch-interior states. A small residual alone neither
certifies a local minimizer nor excludes another stationary point. Such a
claim would additionally require an appropriate second-order or global
argument, which we make only when it is verified for the case in question.
The symbol $P_k(L_\ell)$ denotes degree-$k$ Lagrange elements on mesh level
$L_\ell$.

\subsection{Element and constitutive differentiation}
\label{subsec:element-constitutive-equivalence}

Element AD applies first- and second-order differentiation directly to
$\Phi_e$. At a fixed state $x_\star$, write
$\beta_{eq}^\star:=\beta_{eq}(x_\star;\theta)$. Constitutive AD differentiates
the same selected scalar density at the same quadrature jet. We define
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  z_{eq}^\star
    &:=z_{eq}(x_\star;\theta),\\
  \sigma_{eq}^\star
    &:=\partial_z
      \mathcal{W}_{\beta_{eq}^\star}
      (z_{eq}^\star;m_{eq}),\\
  C_{eq}^\star
    &:=\partial^2_{zz}
      \mathcal{W}_{\beta_{eq}^\star}
      (z_{eq}^\star;m_{eq}).
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:constitutive-objects}
\end{equation}
Here $\sigma_{eq}^\star$ denotes the vector conjugate to the complete local jet;
it is stress-like when that jet is a strain or deformation gradient. The
branch-stability assumption below makes
$\beta_{eq}(x;\theta)=\beta_{eq}^\star$ on a neighborhood of $x_\star$; the selector
is therefore locally constant during branch-interior differentiation.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-functional branchwise equivalence]
\label{prop:branchwise-route-equivalence}
Fix $x_\star\in\mathcal{D}_h(\theta)$. Assume that, on a neighborhood of
$x_\star$,
(i) both routes use the same scalar densities, quadrature weights, material and
history data; (ii) $u_e(x)=A_e x+b_e$ is affine; (iii) every $B_{eq}$ is fixed;
(iv) each selected density is twice continuously differentiable; (v) the
branch labels and principal-value ordering do not change; and (vi) both routes
use the same tensor and engineering-shear conventions. Then element AD and
constitutive AD give identical element residuals and Hessians in exact
arithmetic:
\begin{align}
  \nabla_x\Phi_e(x_\star;\theta)
  &=\sum_q w_{eq}A_e^\top B_{eq}^\top\sigma_{eq}^\star,
  \nonumber\\
  \nabla_x^2\Phi_e(x_\star;\theta)
  &=\sum_q w_{eq}A_e^\top B_{eq}^\top
    C_{eq}^\star B_{eq}A_e.
  \label{eq:branchwise-route-identity}
\end{align}
Consequently, assembling either set of element objects gives the same global
free-space gradient and Hessian of \eqref{eq:generic-potential}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The affine map has zero second derivative. Applying the chain rule first to
$z_{eq}(x;\theta)$ and then to every summand of
\eqref{eq:element-quadrature-potential} gives
\eqref{eq:branchwise-route-identity}. Summation and the fixed free-space
assembly map preserve the identities.
\end{proof}

In particular, the linear external load contributes a constant gradient and
no Hessian. The assembled derivatives at $x_\star$ are therefore
\begin{align}
  g_h(x_\star;\theta)
  &=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{T}_h}\sum_q
    w_{eq}A_e^\top B_{eq}^\top\sigma_{eq}^\star-f_h(\theta),
  \nonumber\\
  H_h(x_\star;\theta)
  &=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{T}_h}\sum_q
    w_{eq}A_e^\top B_{eq}^\top C_{eq}^\star B_{eq}A_e,
  \label{eq:assembled-gradient-hessian}
\end{align}
because
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_x\bigl[-f_h(\theta)^\top x\bigr]=-f_h(\theta),
  \qquad
  \nabla_x^2\bigl[-f_h(\theta)^\top x\bigr]=0.
  \label{eq:external-load-derivatives}
\end{equation}

Proposition~\ref{prop:branchwise-route-equivalence} is conditional. It does not
apply at a branch switch, under an unresolved principal-value ordering, or when
the compared codes change quadrature or constraints. It also establishes the
derivatives of the implemented scalar potential, not equivalence to an
incremental return-mapping model.

\subsection{Colored sparse recovery}
\label{subsec:colored-recovery}

Let $H:=H_h(x;\theta)$ and let
$\rho:\{1,\ldots,n\}\to\{1,\ldots,N_{\mathrm{rank}}\}$ assign every global
free row to one mathematical owner. We set $I_r:=\rho^{-1}(r)$ and let
$\mathcal{P}_r$ contain the structural entries $(i,j)$ of those rows.
The local element set $\mathcal{E}_r$ contains every element incident on
$I_r$, and $J_r$ contains every free column appearing in those elements.  We
write $H^{(r)}$ for the Hessian of the sum over $\mathcal{E}_r$, restricted to
$J_r$.  Because all element contributions incident on an owned row are present,
$H^{(r)}_{ij}=H_{ij}$ for $(i,j)\in\mathcal{P}_r$.

The rank-local column-interference graph joins columns $j,k\in J_r$ whenever
some owned row $i\in I_r$ satisfies
$(i,j),(i,k)\in\mathcal{P}_r$. A local color class
$\mathcal{C}_{r,c}$ defines the overlap seed
\begin{equation}
  v_{r,c}:=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{C}_{r,c}}e_j,
  \qquad y_{r,c}:=H^{(r)}v_{r,c}.
  \label{eq:colored-seed}
\end{equation}
This construction is the direct Hessian analogue of classical sparse
derivative probing \citep{curtis1974sparsejacobian,coleman1984sparsehessian}.

\begin{proposition}[Owned-row colored recovery]
\label{prop:owned-row-colored-recovery}
For every rank $r$, assume that (i) $\mathcal{P}_r$ contains every active
nonzero $(i,j)$ of each owned row; (ii) $\mathcal{E}_r$ contains every element
contributing to those rows, with complete state, affine-lift, material, and
selected-branch data on its overlap; (iii) $J_r$ contains every column in
$\mathcal{P}_r$, and each owned row contains at most one structural entry from
any $\mathcal{C}_{r,c}$; and (iv) $H^{(r)}v_{r,c}$ is evaluated exactly with
the complete overlap seed. Then, only for $(i,j)\in\mathcal{P}_r$ with
$j\in\mathcal{C}_{r,c}$, assigning
$H_{ij}:=(y_{r,c})_i$ recovers every owned structural entry exactly. The union
of the owned rows is the canonical distributed matrix $H$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For an owned row $i$, the identity
$(y_{r,c})_i=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{C}_{r,c}}H^{(r)}_{ij}$ contains at most one
structural nonzero by assumption (iii). Assumptions (i), (ii), and (iv) make
the local sum complete and give $H^{(r)}_{ij}=H_{ij}$ on the owned pattern.
The restricted assignment therefore recovers that entry, and unique ownership
assembles each recovered row once.
\end{proof}

In the implementation, the nonoverlapping PETSc row ranges realize the map
$\rho$. Proposition~\ref{prop:owned-row-colored-recovery} depends only on the
unique mathematical assignment, not on this particular library convention.

\begin{proposition}[Component-lifted scalar coloring]
\label{prop:component-lifted-coloring}
Let $S$ be the scalar finite-element support and suppose that the structural
pattern of a $d$-component vector Hessian is a subpattern of
$S\otimes\mathbf{1}_{d\times d}$. If $c_s$ is a column-intersection coloring
of $S$, then
\begin{equation}
  c(a,k):=d\,c_s(a)+k,
  \qquad k\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\},
  \label{eq:component-lifted-color}
\end{equation}
is a valid column-intersection coloring of every constrained vector subpattern.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Suppose that vector columns $(a,k)$ and $(b,\ell)$ meet one structural row. If
$a\ne b$, then scalar columns $a$ and $b$ meet the corresponding scalar support
row, so $c_s(a)\ne c_s(b)$. If $a=b$, distinct vector columns have
$k\ne\ell$. Equation~\eqref{eq:component-lifted-color} therefore assigns
different colors in both cases. Removing constrained columns cannot introduce
a conflict.
\end{proof}

The proposition permits different rank-local color labels and counts because
each rank evaluates its own complete incident-element Hessian and inserts only
its owned pattern. It does not permit a missing incident element or structural
edge, incomplete state or seed overlap, inconsistent branch data, or two
same-color columns that meet an owned row. The implemented recovery uses AD
Hessian-vector products; a finite-difference gradient probe would add its
truncation error.

\subsection{Cost components and comparison variables}
\label{subsec:cost-model}

Let $m_e$ be the nominal number of local element coefficient slots, including
slots whose values are fixed by essential conditions. Furthermore, let $s$ be
the constitutive dimension, $n_q$ the quadrature-point count,
$n_{e,r}^{\mathrm{loc}}$ the overlap-element count on rank $r$, and $c_r$ its
local color count. Because the measured response is an MPI collective maximum,
the structural work proxy must describe the busiest rank rather than the global
number of uniquely owned elements. We therefore define
\begin{align}
  \chi_{\mathrm{elem}}
  &:=\max_r n_{e,r}^{\mathrm{loc}}n_qm_e^2,
  \nonumber\\
  \chi_{\mathrm{color}}
  &:=\max_r\bigl(n_{e,r}^{\mathrm{loc}}c_r\bigr)n_qm_e,
  \nonumber\\
  \chi_{\mathrm{const}}
  &:=\max_r n_{e,r}^{\mathrm{loc}}n_q
    \bigl(s^2+s^2m_e+sm_e^2\bigr).
  \label{eq:route-work-proxies}
\end{align}
The first two expressions count the dense element-Hessian contribution shape
and the vector work propagated by the exact AD-HVP seeds, respectively. In the
constitutive expression, $s^2$ counts the dense tangent $C_{eq}$, while
$s^2m_e$ and $sm_e^2$ count the two contractions used to form
$B_{eq}^\top C_{eq}B_{eq}$.
The proxies in \eqref{eq:route-work-proxies} are structural operation-shape
counts, not exact models of operation counts, cache behavior, or compiler cost.
For warm fixed-state measurement $j$, let $T_{rj}^{\mathrm{wall}}$ be the outer
tangent-construction wall time on rank $r$. The primary observation and its
within-process summary are
\begin{equation}
  T_j^{\mathrm{wall}}:=\max_r T_{rj}^{\mathrm{wall}},
  \qquad
  T:=\operatorname{median}_{j=1,\ldots,5}T_j^{\mathrm{wall}}.
  \label{eq:route-wall-time-response}
\end{equation}
The implementation also records cumulative callback diagnostics for Hessian
actions, extraction, insertion, and communication. These counters include
compilation, warmup, and measured calls; hence they are neither an additive
partition of $T$ nor confirmatory phase timings. Architecture and working-set
descriptors serve as cache covariates, while $\iota_e$ and $\iota_o$, the
element and overlap max-to-mean ratios, serve as imbalance covariates. We also
record matrix nonzeros, overlap size, branch fractions, rank count, and the
applicable proxy from \eqref{eq:route-work-proxies}; peak memory remains an
outcome. A prespecified synthetic factor diagnostic varies these mechanisms one
at a time. Because its kernels are not route-faithful, it can support only
descriptive mechanism checks.

The confirmatory cost analysis is prespecified; we do not fit it to the present
local evidence. Let $I_a$ indicate route $a\in\mathcal{A}$, where
$\mathcal{A}:=\{\mathrm{elem},\allowbreak\mathrm{color},\allowbreak
\mathrm{const}\}$, and let $K$ indicate the cluster architecture. We set
\begin{equation}
  \chi:=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}I_a\chi_a,
  \label{eq:selected-route-work-proxy}
\end{equation}
where the three route-specific proxies are defined in
\eqref{eq:route-work-proxies}. We denote
the maximum owned matrix-nonzero count by $n_{\mathrm{nz}}^{\max}$ and the
maximum overlap-DOF count by $n_{\mathrm{ov}}^{\max}$. The remaining
variables are the MPI rank count $R$, the plastic quadrature fraction
$p_{\mathrm{pl}}$, and the imbalance ratios $\iota_e,\iota_o$ defined above.
If the complete cluster-backed design becomes admissible, the prespecified
no-intercept model for the collective-max warm-median
tangent-construction time is
\begin{align}
  \log T
  ={}&\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\alpha_a I_a
    +\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\kappa_a K I_a
    +\beta_\chi\log(1+\chi)
    +\beta_{\mathrm{nz}}\log(1+n_{\mathrm{nz}}^{\max})
  \nonumber\\
   &+\beta_{\mathrm{ov}}\log(1+n_{\mathrm{ov}}^{\max})
    +\beta_R\log R+\beta_p p_{\mathrm{pl}}
    +\beta_e\iota_e+\beta_o\iota_o.
  \label{eq:route-time-model}
\end{align}
The route indicators supply the three levels. Complete block medians would be
equally weighted; censored or incomplete groups would be excluded without
imputation. Workstation rank 1 and cluster ranks 1 and 8 are reserved for
training, while cluster rank 32 is an untouched holdout. The frozen protocol also specifies
minimum sample counts, design-rank and conditioning checks, prediction and
ordering criteria, and paired block-bootstrap intervals. Unless those
requirements and the derivative, state, endpoint, and provenance gates all
pass, we estimate no coefficients and make no selector or crossover claim.
Section~\ref{sec:performance-solver-behavior} records the current
insufficiency.

\subsection{Mesh-aware nonlinear accuracy}
\label{subsec:mesh-aware-stopping}

Coefficient norms change with mesh density and therefore do not define a
portable stopping contract. Let $M_h$ be a symmetric positive-definite Riesz
operator on the constrained free space. We use the primal and dual norms
\begin{equation}
  \lVert s\rVert_{M_h}:=(s^\top M_hs)^{1/2},
  \qquad
  \lVert g\rVert_{M_h^{-1}}:=(g^\top M_h^{-1}g)^{1/2}.
  \label{eq:riesz-primal-dual-norms}
\end{equation}
The scalar problems use a row-sum lumped P1 mass operator. Hyperelasticity and
the branch-structured mechanics problem use the tangent of a reference elastic
energy on the identical constrained free space. Before a reference tangent is
admitted, a scale-aware symmetry check and a recorded direct factorization
report zero negative and zero numerical zero pivots under the stated solver
options. This is a numerical inertia check, not an analytic SPD proof. Each
inverse-Riesz solve also passes an independently recomputed true-residual gate.

For nonlinear iteration $k\geq0$, let $g_k:=g_h(x_k;\theta)$ and define
\begin{equation}
  R_k:=\lVert g_k\rVert_{M_h^{-1}},\qquad
  S_k:=\lVert x_k\rVert_{M_h}.
  \label{eq:absolute-residual-state}
\end{equation}
For $k\geq1$, we additionally define
\begin{equation}
  C_k:=\lVert x_k-x_{k-1}\rVert_{M_h},\qquad
  D_k:=\lvert\Pi_h(x_k;\theta)-\Pi_h(x_{k-1};\theta)\rvert.
  \label{eq:absolute-residual-correction}
\end{equation}
We report the dimensionless quantities
\begin{equation}
  r_k^{\mathrm{rel}}:=\frac{R_k}{R_0},
  \qquad
  c_k^{\mathrm{rel}}:=\frac{C_k}{\max(s_0,S_k)},
  \label{eq:relative-residual-correction}
\end{equation}
where $r_k^{\mathrm{rel}}$ is defined only when $R_0>0$. The scale $s_0>0$
has the same units as the primal norm. It may equal $S_0$ when $S_0>0$;
otherwise, an explicit positive scale must be prescribed independently.

The Tier-B branch-structured solves use a gradient-only, initial-relative
criterion. For polynomial degree $p\in\{1,4\}$, let
\begin{equation}
  \tau_{R,p}^{\mathrm{rel}}
  :=\begin{cases}
       10^{-6}, & p=1,\\
       10^{-7}, & p=4.
     \end{cases}
  \label{eq:tier-b-relative-targets}
\end{equation}
The terminal Boolean is
\begin{equation}
  T_{k,p}^{\mathrm{B}}
  :=\{R_0>0\}\land
    \left\{r_k^{\mathrm{rel}}
      \leq\tau_{R,p}^{\mathrm{rel}}\right\}.
  \label{eq:terminal-boolean-contract}
\end{equation}
The absolute residual tolerance is zero, so no absolute fallback modifies
\eqref{eq:terminal-boolean-contract}. An iterate may terminate a Tier-B solve
only after it is accepted and all required terminal data are finite, but these
run-validity conditions are not alternative convergence measures. If $R_0=0$,
the ratio is undefined and the solve lies outside this relative-only contract;
such an initially stationary case must be documented separately.

The quantities $D_k$, $C_k$, $S_k$, and $c_k^{\mathrm{rel}}$ are reported as
diagnostics. In particular, the recorded correction reference
$c_k^{\mathrm{rel}}\leq 2\times10^{-3}$ neither terminates nor rejects a
Tier-B solve, and no energy-change threshold enters
\eqref{eq:terminal-boolean-contract}. The endpoint analysis independently
recomputes $R_k$ and verifies the selected branch, Riesz solve, state identity,
and provenance; these admission checks do not alter the gradient-only stopping
rule.

For hyperelasticity, the coefficient vector represents the free part of the
deformation map under one fixed affine lift. Its reference-energy norm is used
only within that representation and is not invariant under a change of lift.
The endpoint record contains the selected Riesz operator, scale, free-space
identity, numerical inertia record, inverse-solve diagnostics, and independently
recomputed terminal norms.

The Riesz operators and tolerances are case-specific. The resulting residuals
permit comparisons within the stated free-space representation; they do not
establish a universal mesh-independent stopping threshold.

\subsection{Evidence and timing contract}
\label{subsec:evidence-contract}

Correctness precedes time. A fixed-state route row first passes the exact state
identity, the common element-AD residual, four prescribed tangent actions,
branch, and structural-covariate checks. The common residual verifies the state
and shared assembly path; it is not an independent constitutive-gradient test.
At the feasible one-rank $P_1$ points, the row also passes a direct full-CSR
comparison. A Tier-B full-solve row additionally passes the initial-relative
Riesz residual, independent Riesz true-residual, endpoint-equivalence, selected
branch, and provenance gates. Energy-change and correction quantities remain
diagnostics and are not route-admission gates.

The fixed-state timing design uses one compilation/warmup assembly, five warm
measurements, balanced route order, collective-max wall times, and independent
allocation blocks. Tier B instead uses ten cold-process paired blocks per route
and rank, with no embedded warmup. Both designs retain explicit censoring. The
full-solve records keep compilation, setup, assembly, preconditioner
construction, Krylov solution, and total time as distinct diagnostics, without
asserting that independently reduced quantities form an additive partition. A
capped or failed solve is not encoded as a large successful time.

This hierarchy yields four distinct statements: a proved identity under stated
assumptions; verification on finite tested states; a descriptive finite route
map; and an empirical crossover or selector. Evidence for one level does not
imply the next. In particular, near-equal energy does not replace a residual or
tangent comparison, and one tangent probe does not establish complete matrix
equality.
