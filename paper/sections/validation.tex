\label{sec:validation}

This section separates validation and external-comparison evidence from the
performance results that follow. The distinction is important because the
paper uses different levels of evidence for different claims: Hyperelasticity
has a narrow external comparison against JAX-FEM, while
\plasticitythree{} is evaluated through a matched endpoint-comparator
formulation for the surrogate studied here.

\subsection{Comparison Metrics}
\label{sec:val-metrics}

For vector and field comparisons with nonzero reference norm, we report
relative differences of the form
\begin{equation}
  \delta_M(a,b)
  =
  \frac{\|a-b\|_M}
       {\|a\|_M},
  \label{eq:validation-relative-difference}
\end{equation}
where $a$ is the reference observable, $b$ is the compared observable, and $M$
denotes the discrete norm used for the comparison. For nodal displacement
vectors, $M$ is the identity on the compared free degrees of freedom. More
generally, $\|r\|_M=(r^\top M r)^{1/2}$, with diagonal quadrature weights for
field samples and the stated sample/profile weights for curves. The reported
field and profile observables have nonzero reference norms. Scalar work and
energy differences use the same
reference-denominator convention; the hyperelastic energy post-comparison uses
an absolute denominator floor of $10^{-12}$. We write
$u_{\max}$ for the maximum displacement magnitude over the compared state.
Curve metrics use Euclidean weights on ordered samples. The hyperelastic
centerline samples $u_x$ at reference nodes with $y=z=0$, ordered by $x$. The
\plasticitythree{} boundary profile samples displacement magnitudes on reference
nodes within 4\% of the domain $y$-span about the mid-$y$ plane and above the
0.6 reference-$z$ quantile, ordered by $x$; the candidate profile is linearly
interpolated to the reference $x$ coordinates before computing the relative
$L^2$ difference.

The hyperelastic JAX-FEM comparison uses a 5\% predefined threshold for the
final energy, Euclidean nodal full-field displacement, centerline displacement, and
$u_{\max}$-curve differences. The \plasticitythree{} fixed-$\lambda_{\mathrm{sr}}$
matched-comparator diagnostic uses three predefined criteria: at most $0.03$ relative
difference in the highest successful strength-reduction factor, at most $0.05$ relative
$L^2$ difference for the $u_{\max}(\lambda_{\mathrm{sr}})$ curve, and at most $0.10$
relative $L^2$ difference for the endpoint displacement. The deviatoric-strain
and boundary-profile differences in \Cref{tab:plasticity3d-validation} are
diagnostics, not acceptance criteria.
These thresholds are predeclared engineering agreement gates for companion and
surrogate comparisons. They are not literature validation tolerances and do not
replace path-history validation for elastoplastic evolution.

\subsection{Hyperelasticity External Comparison}
\label{sec:val-hyperelasticity}

The hyperelasticity energy is defined in \Cref{sec:bench-he}. The external
comparison here is deliberately narrow and uses a serial companion case rather
than the 24-step rotating benchmark path. Both implementations use the same
level-1 mesh and the same right-face $x$-translation schedule
$d=(0.0025,0.005,0.0075,0.01)$, then post-compare both terminal states with
the energy in \eqref{eq:hyperelasticity-energy}. This supports terminal-state
observable agreement for this hyperelastic companion problem only. The comparison is
therefore an energy-postprocessed terminal-state check for one hyperelastic
case, and it does not assert constitutive-law identity between the two
implementations: the specific JAX-FEM companion case used here uses a
logarithmic-$J$ Piola form, whereas \eqref{eq:hyperelasticity-energy} uses the
$D_1(J-1)^2$ volumetric penalty.

\subsection{Plasticity3D Endpoint-Surrogate Comparison}
\label{sec:val-plasticity3d}

The \plasticitythree{} benchmark is tied to Mohr--Coulomb slope-stability
reference formulations, but the object studied here is the algorithmic
endpoint surrogate defined in
\Cref{sec:bench-plasticity3d} by
\eqref{eq:plasticity3d-discrete} and \eqref{eq:plasticity3d-potential}.
The Sysala return-mapping, optimization, convex-optimization, and advanced
continuation papers provide reference-model and method context for this problem
class
\citep{sysala2017returnmapping,sysala2021optimization,sysala2025convexoptimization,sysala2025advancedcontinuation}.
The efficient \v{C}erm{\'a}k--Sysala--Valdman MATLAB elastoplastic
implementation of \citet{cermak2019efficient} provides implementation context
for practical two- and three-dimensional elastoplastic finite-element solvers.
None of these sources is
used here as a verified numerical baseline.

The endpoint-surrogate comparison therefore uses two diagnostics. Here the
matched endpoint comparator means the endpoint formulation used for these
observable comparisons, not the cited incremental-history studies. The
comparator uses the same endpoint functional, mesh, material table, Davis-B
strength-reduction schedule, boundary conditions, and active free degrees
of freedom, but evaluates the endpoint observables through an independent
reference-formula assembly path. The endpoint-observable check tests a seven-step
direct strength-reduction branch,
$\lambda_{\mathrm{sr}}=\num{1.0},\num{1.1},\ldots,\num{1.6}$, against the matched endpoint
comparator on the $P_2(L_1)$ comparator case. The work observable is the external
work $\omega=f_{\mathrm{ext}}^\top u$ reported by the two endpoint
formulations. Its relative difference is \num{3.877e-5}, the displacement
relative $L^2$ difference is \num{3.517e-3}, and the deviatoric-strain relative
$L^2$ difference is \num{8.720e-3}.

The fixed-$\lambda_{\mathrm{sr}}$ matched-comparator diagnostic asks a narrower
fixed-$\lambda_{\mathrm{sr}}$ question against the matched endpoint comparator.
After aligning the comparator boundary conditions with the active boundary
condition set, the fixed-$\lambda_{\mathrm{sr}}$ grid is capped at the
main-figure strength-reduction value
$\lambda_{\mathrm{sr}}=\num{1.55}$. Here
$\lambda_{\max}^{\mathrm{succ}}$ denotes the largest value in the tested grid
$\{\num{1.0},\num{1.2},\num{1.4},\num{1.5},\num{1.55}\}$ that satisfies the relative-correction stopping
criterion. The comparison matches $\lambda_{\max}^{\mathrm{succ}}$ on that grid
and satisfies the predefined criteria for the
$u_{\max}(\lambda_{\mathrm{sr}})$ curve and endpoint displacement: the
$u_{\max}(\lambda_{\mathrm{sr}})$ relative $L^2$ difference is
\num{6.396e-6}, and the endpoint displacement relative $L^2$ difference is
\num{8.299e-4}. The endpoint deviatoric-strain relative $L^2$ difference
(\num{4.307e-3}) and boundary-profile relative $L^2$ difference
(\num{5.229e-4}) are reported as diagnostics rather than acceptance criteria.
This remains an endpoint check rather than a path-consistent plastic-history
reference.
