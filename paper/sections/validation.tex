\label{sec:validation}

We verify the discrete formulation in stages. Independent manufactured and
analytic solutions test the energies and spatial discretizations, fixed-state
comparisons test the derivative routes, and canonical MPI comparisons test the
distributed algebraic construction. None of these experiments is used as
timing evidence.

\subsection{Comparison metrics and admission gates}
\label{subsec:verification-normalization}

For two scalars, vectors, or matrices of the same physical type, we define the
relative defect and the matrix-symmetry defect by
\begin{equation}
  \delta(a,b):=
  \begin{cases}
    0, & \lVert a\rVert=\lVert b\rVert=0,\\
    \displaystyle\frac{\lVert a-b\rVert}
      {\max(\lVert a\rVert,\lVert b\rVert)}, & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
  \qquad
  \delta_{\mathrm{sym}}(A):=
  \begin{cases}
    0, & \lVert A\rVert_{\mathrm F}=0,\\
    \displaystyle\frac{\lVert A-A^\top\rVert_{\mathrm F}}
      {\lVert A\rVert_{\mathrm F}}, & \text{otherwise}.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:verification-relative-defects}
\end{equation}
Here $\lVert\cdot\rVert$ denotes absolute value for scalars, the Euclidean norm
for vectors, and the Frobenius norm for matrices. We also report absolute
defects whenever either object vanishes. The canonical MPI comparison uses the
nondimensional floor $\max(1,\lVert a\rVert,\lVert b\rVert)$ in its stored
relative discrepancies; this convention prevents division by a nearly zero
reference and is not reused as a physical endpoint norm.

The $p$-Laplace, Ginzburg--Landau, and nonaffine hyperelastic Newton solves use
problem-specific relative weak-residual tolerances $10^{-8}$, $10^{-9}$, and
$10^{-10}$, respectively. In each case, the reported ratio is
$\lVert R_{h,\mathrm{free}}^{(k)}\rVert_2/
 \lVert R_{h,\mathrm{free}}^{(0)}\rVert_2$.
We approximate the spatial errors in the $L^2$ norm and the $H^1$ seminorm by
quadrature. The mechanics problem additionally uses the $L^2$ norm of the
first-Piola stress error.

For a unit direction $d$, the centered Hessian-action check is
\begin{equation}
  D_hg(x;d):=\frac{g(x+hd)-g(x-hd)}{2h},
  \qquad \delta\bigl(D_hg(x;d),H(x)d\bigr).
  \label{eq:centered-action-check}
\end{equation}
The smooth-energy, fixed-element endpoint-surrogate, and material-point checks use
$h=3\times10^{-5}$, $10^{-7}$, and $10^{-6}$, respectively. Their prescribed
action tolerance is $10^{-7}$; analytic route defects must not exceed
$10^{-8}$, and smooth-branch symmetry defects must not exceed $10^{-10}$.
Complete step sweeps are retained rather than fitting their roundoff tails.

For branch-interior tests, each predicate is divided by a quantity with the
same units. Yield predicates use the maximum of the cohesion, trial value, and
elastic stress terms. Multiplier and interface predicates use the maximum of
the principal strains, return multipliers, return tests, and
$\bar c_\lambda/\max(|\mu|,|K|,|\lambda_{\mathrm L}|)$. The active margin is
the smallest signed normalized inequality required by the selected first-match
branch; strict interiors require a margin of at least $10^{-3}$.

\subsection{Manufactured scalar problems}
\label{subsec:manufactured-verification}

The $p$-Laplace problem reaches the relative weak-residual gate on all four
meshes in three damped Newton steps. The successive $L^2$ orders are $1.986$,
$1.996$, and $1.999$, while the $H^1$-seminorm orders are $0.990$, $0.998$,
and $0.999$. The finest-mesh errors are $2.76\times10^{-5}$ and
$5.45\times10^{-3}$, respectively. The minimum discrete gradient norm remains
above $1.19$, so this test avoids the zero-gradient degeneracy of the $p=3$
Hessian.

The controlled Ginzburg--Landau branch converges in four Newton steps on every
mesh. Its finest-pair orders are $2.001$ in $L^2$ and $1.000$ in the $H^1$
seminorm, and all nodal values lie between $0.800$ and $0.902$. The observed
rates are consistent with the expected $P_1$ behavior on this selected smooth
branch. They do not independently verify the quadrature rule or establish basin
robustness for the zero-source double-well problem.

\subsection{Hyperelastic verification}
\label{sec:val-hyperelasticity}

For the affine patch, the relative energy, residual, and Hessian discrepancies
are $4.58\times10^{-15}$, $2.67\times10^{-15}$, and
$4.38\times10^{-16}$, respectively. The relative boundary-traction imbalance
is $1.75\times10^{-16}$. The net internal-force norm is
$1.92\times10^{-16}$, and the largest rigid-translation Hessian-action norm is
$1.19\times10^{-15}$ in the nondimensional force units of the unit-cube test.
A superposed rotation produces zero stored-energy defect and a
$7.94\times10^{-17}$ first-Piola covariance error.

The nonaffine manufactured problem uses 4, 8, 16, and 24 subdivisions per
axis, and all four meshes converge in four damped Newton steps. The last-pair
orders are $1.887$ for displacement in $L^2$, $1.006$ for the deformation
gradient, and $0.983$ for first-Piola stress. The minimum computed determinant
is $0.844$, and the maximum tangent-symmetry defect is
$9.99\times10^{-17}$. Across all mesh levels and the three response norms, the
largest order-4-versus-order-6 solution difference is
$8.29\times10^{-6}$ times the corresponding finite-element error. The order-8
calculation separately confirms stabilization of the assembled load vector.
These results support consistency on the tested orientation-preserving
trajectory, but not a general nonlinear convergence rate.

\subsection{Derivative and branch checks}
\label{subsec:derivative-verification}

The smooth fixed-element block contains linear, nonlinear, indefinite, and
admissible finite-strain energies. Across its five cases, the maximum analytic
gradient and Hessian discrepancies are $1.29\times10^{-15}$ and
$2.23\times10^{-16}$. The $P_1$, $P_2$, and $P_4$ endpoint-surrogate blocks each
contain five prescribed element states and pass their exact CSR-structure gates.
Across these blocks, the maximum residual and Hessian relative errors are
$2.35\times10^{-16}$ and $2.34\times10^{-16}$, respectively. For $P_4$
alone, the corresponding residual, Hessian, and symmetry maxima are
$2.26\times10^{-16}$, $2.34\times10^{-16}$, and
$1.24\times10^{-16}$.

The material-point matrix contains five strict branch interiors, 15 two-sided
interface pairs, 15 rotated interiors, and seven repeated-principal-value
cases. All finiteness, symmetry, transcription, rotation, and directional
gates pass. Across the five strict interiors, the largest centered
Hessian-action error at the prescribed step is $1.54\times10^{-9}$, and the
maximum Hessian-symmetry defect is $8.68\times10^{-17}$. The two-sided probes
exhibit order-one changes in the selected Hessian. They illustrate why the
smooth-branch assumptions of
Proposition~\ref{prop:branchwise-route-equivalence} exclude branch interfaces;
the finite probes do not prove a general impossibility result there.

\subsection{Canonical distributed equivalence}
\label{subsec:distributed-verification}

The one-rank hyperelastic reference and the two- and four-rank constructions
agree exactly in topology, maps, state, direction, and CSR structure. The
maximum nondimensional energy, residual, matrix, tangent-action, and linear-
correction discrepancies are $8.67\times10^{-19}$, zero, zero,
$3.35\times10^{-16}$, and $2.33\times10^{-16}$, respectively. This verifies
rank-local element-Hessian assembly at one prescribed state; it does not test a
nonlinear endpoint or parallel efficiency.

Table~\ref{tab:distributed-colored-verification} gives an independent check of
distributed colored sparse recovery for $P_1$ and $P_2$, elastic and mixed
states, and one, two, and four ranks. All 12 blocks, comprising 36 route
executions, pass the exact state, common-residual, branch, and prescribed
tangent-action checks. The four one-rank blocks additionally pass exact CSR
pattern and value comparisons; no direct distributed CSR comparison is claimed
for two or four ranks. Before presentation rounding, the largest nonzero
relative discrepancy over the checked tangent actions is
$2.64\times10^{-16}$. The result establishes fixed-state correctness over this
finite test matrix; it provides neither a scaling result nor an endpoint
comparison.

\subsection{Scope of the endpoint-surrogate checks}
\label{sec:val-plasticity3d}

The endpoint-surrogate experiments verify the scalar branch program and its
derivatives, not an independent constitutive model. The NumPy material-point
code independently implements rotations, finite differences, and interface
construction, but it transcribes the same endpoint energy. We therefore treat
the result as internal verification. Physical validation would require a
matched incremental return map and a recognized boundary-value benchmark with
common history, constraints, and material assumptions.

The same limitation applies at repeated principal values. The deterministic
tie convention prevents nonfinite automatic-differentiation values and selects
a reproducible branch. It does not make the ordinary Hessian unique at an
exact eigenvalue degeneracy.
