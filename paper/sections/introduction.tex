Nonlinear finite-element models in mechanics, scalar PDEs, and design
optimization often combine three computational difficulties. The local model may
be easiest to write as an energy or constitutive law whose derivatives are
tedious to assemble by hand. The global problem then requires robust nonlinear
globalization and sparse Newton--Krylov linear algebra. Finally, the
implementation must remain comparable across reference formulations,
derivative approximations, and distributed-memory solver choices. A useful
computational toolset for this class of problems should therefore expose not
only a modeling interface, but also the derivative route, assembly layout,
nonlinear solver policy, and preconditioner design.

We develop and evaluate this design point through a solver-centered toolset for
nonlinear finite-element energy solves and optimization. In the primary
\jaxpetsc{} realization, JAX represents local energies and constitutive laws,
while PETSc owns sparse vectors and matrices, nonlinear globalization, Krylov
methods, and preconditioners. The \purejax{} and \fenics{} formulations provide
scoped reference implementations where they are available, and external
comparisons are restricted to explicitly matched benchmark definitions and
observables. These matched reference checks let us study derivative and solver
choices for nonlinear energy minimization without ranking full software
ecosystems.

The surrounding literature separates into high-level FEM and adjoint
automation, JAX-native differentiable FEM, bridge architectures that combine
JAX-style local differentiation with established solver environments, and
parallel topology-optimization workflows. \Cref{sec:related} gives the detailed
account. The positioning needed here is narrower: we study a
JAX-local/PETSc-global toolset for sparse nonlinear energy solves, with
comparison claims limited to the matched definitions and observables stated in
\Cref{sec:validation}.

\subsection{Contributions}
\label{sec:intro-contributions}

The contribution is an implemented \jaxpetsc{} scientific toolset for nonlinear
FEM energy minimization, organized around three solver components:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Globalized energy minimization.} We compare line-search,
        trust-region, and hybrid globalization choices on representative
        nonlinear finite-element benchmarks, and we keep the reported
        conclusions tied to the tested benchmark definitions.
  \item \textbf{Derivative construction in sparse assembly.} We implement and compare
        element automatic differentiation, constitutive automatic
        differentiation, and colored sparse finite-difference recovery inside
        the same sparse assembly framework.
  \item \textbf{Distributed PETSc realization.} We connect the nonlinear and
        derivative choices to PETSc Krylov methods and preconditioners,
        including Hypre, GAMG, and problem-specific polynomial multigrid
        policies used in the larger mechanics and topology runs.
\end{enumerate}

Together, these components make derivative construction, sparse assembly,
globalization, and preconditioning part of one numerical-method choice rather
than separate implementation details.

We evaluate these components on six benchmark families: $p$-Laplace, scalar
Ginzburg--Landau, hyperelasticity, two- and three-dimensional
Mohr--Coulomb-type plasticity, and topology optimization. The scalar problems
isolate formulation and derivative agreement. Hyperelasticity adds
finite-strain mechanics and a narrow JAX-FEM companion comparison. The
plasticity benchmarks test nonsmooth constitutive structure,
reference-observable agreement, and multigrid policy. Topology optimization
tests a distributed design-and-mechanics loop. The geomechanics context follows
the finite-element strength-reduction and 3D slope-stability literature
\citep{tschuchnigg2015nonassociated,sysala2017returnmapping,sysala2021optimization,sysala2025convexoptimization,sysala2025advancedcontinuation},
but the plasticity object studied here is explicitly an algorithmic endpoint
surrogate, not a claim of path-consistent incremental-history equivalence.

Three comparisons matter most for the narrative that follows. First, relative to
high-level FEM and adjoint ecosystems
\citep{logg2012fenicsbook,baratta2025dolfinx,farrell2013dolfinadjoint,mitusch2019pyadjoint,blauth2023cashocsv2},
the present toolset moves the center of gravity from PDE-level adjoint-centered
automation toward distributed nonlinear solve policy and multiple second-order
routes inside the same sparse solver stack. Second, relative to JAX-native
mechanics and differentiable-simulation frameworks
\citep{xue2023jaxfem,xue2026implicit,bode2025autopdex,hu2025jaxcpfem}, the main
distinction is that the implementation here centers PETSc-owned sparse MPI data
structures and preconditioned Newton solves. JAX-FEM and JAX-CPFEM emphasize
JAX-level mechanics modeling and GPU-oriented differentiable simulation; the
later Xue finite-element work adds implicit Hessian-vector products for inverse
problems; and AutoPDEx adds nonlinear minimizers, implicit differentiation, and
optional PETSc integration.
Third, relative to recent bridge and hybrid papers that combine
JAX-style differentiation with established FEM or solver environments
\citep{yashchuk2023bringing,latyshev2025externaloperators,cattaneo2026jetsci},
the paper's emphasis is on extending those bridge architectures into
benchmarked PETSc sparse assembly, Krylov solves, and nonlinear
globalization for benchmark families that include topology optimization and 3D
Mohr--Coulomb-type mechanics.

The comparison in \Cref{tab:sota-comparison} therefore places the present
toolset between high-level FEM and adjoint ecosystems, JAX-native
differentiable mechanics, and recent JAX--solver bridge architectures. Its
distinguishing feature is the joint treatment of local differentiation,
PETSc-owned sparse MPI assembly, nonlinear globalization, and preconditioner
policy across a heterogeneous benchmark suite. The \purejax{}, \fenics{},
JAX-FEM, and reference-model tests serve as scoped checks for specific benchmark
definitions and observables.

implementations used in selected checks.
\Cref{sec:benchmarks} defines the benchmark suite, with each benchmark separated

\Cref{sec:solver-evidence} adds solver-policy diagnostics, followed by a
